\section{Related Work}





\paragraph{\textbf{Open-Domain QA (ODQA)}}

Open-domain question answering studies how to find and use evidence from very large corpora to answer free-form questions. A sequence of datasets marked key shifts in task design: SQuAD \cite{rajpurkarSQuAD100000Questions2016} established span-based reading comprehension; Natural Questions \cite{kwiatkowskiNaturalQuestionsBenchmark2019a} moved to real search queries; TriviaQA \cite{joshiTriviaQALargeScale2017} stressed compositional, trivia-style questions with substantial lexical and syntactic variation; and HotpotQA \cite{yangHotpotQADatasetDiverse2018a} formalized multi-hop reasoning across multiple documents with sentence-level supporting facts and comparison questions, while including distractors to test \emph{focus under noise}. Models such as fusion-in-decoder (FiD) \cite{izacardLeveragingPassageRetrieval2021} showed that aggregating many retrieved passages is a powerful way to synthesize scattered evidence.

Another important dimension in QA is \emph{time}. RealTime QA \cite{kasaiRealTimeQAWhats2024} and FreshQA \cite{vuFreshLLMsRefreshingLarge2023} evaluate whether systems can answer what is true \emph{right now}, revealing failure modes when indices are stale or retrieved content is outdated or contradictory. These settings highlight needs that go beyond span extraction: recognizing when the answer has changed, when a question carries a false premise, and when recent evidence is required over older but higher-ranked documents.

Finally, surface simplicity does not imply task simplicity. SimpleQA \cite{weiMeasuringShortformFactuality2024} demonstrates that short, seemingly straightforward questions can still require precise world knowledge or multi-step reasoning, indicating that difficulty is not well represented by length or phrasing. 



\paragraph{\textbf{Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)}}

Modern RAG approaches follow a common pipeline -- given a query, retrieve top-$k$ passages from a non-parametric memory and condition a generator on them. Foundational formulations include RAG \cite{lewisRetrievalAugmentedGenerationKnowledgeIntensive2020a}, REALM \cite{guuRetrievalAugmentedLanguage2020}, RETRO \cite{borgeaudImprovingLanguageModels2022}, and ATLAS \cite{izacardAtlasFewshotLearning2022}, which together demonstrate that query-time retrieval improves knowledge-intensive tasks while enabling the external memory to be updated without re-training the parametric model. Recent surveys study components and variants of this paradigm \cite{gaoRetrievalAugmentedGenerationLarge2024}.

In the literature, RAG is distinguished from broader tool-augmented generation -- RAG requires \emph{query-conditioned retrieval} whose contents directly condition the generator. Calculators, code execution, or static always-prepended context are typically treated as non-retrieval tools \cite{mialonAugmentedLanguageModels2023}. In practice, the retrieval index can be the open web or enterprise/local corpora, and the mechanism can be lexical (e.g., BM25) or dense; the generator then aggregates evidence across multiple passages \cite{izacardLeveragingPassageRetrieval2021,lewisRetrievalAugmentedGenerationKnowledgeIntensive2020a}.


Beyond single-shot RAG, agentic frameworks let models iteratively issue searches, follow links, and cite sources. WebGPT augments an LLM with browsing and requires collecting references during answering \cite{nakanoWebGPTBrowserassistedQuestionanswering2022a}. ReAct further couples reasoning traces with tool calls (e.g., search, calculator) in a unified prompting loop, enabling multi-hop planning and verification \cite{yaoReActSynergizingReasoning2023a}. These approaches often embed RAG steps inside a broader plan--act--observe loop, allowing complex queries that require cross-document synthesis.


\paragraph{\textbf{Robustness of QA Systems}}

High IID (in-distribution) scores do not guarantee reliability under distribution shift \cite{thakurBEIRHeterogenousBenchmark2021}. Adversarial studies show that small, misleading perturbations can derail readers, revealing reliance on shallow cues \cite{jiaAdversarialExamplesEvaluating2017}. BEIR further documents pronounced \emph{cross-domain} drops for dense retrievers and reader pipelines and highlights BM25’s robustness, with no single model performing uniformly well across 18 heterogeneous datasets \cite{thakurBEIRHeterogenousBenchmark2021}. In realistic open-domain settings, models must also handle noisy or conflicting evidence across retrieved documents. Recent analyses quantify the prevalence of conflicts on the web (\textasciitilde25\% of factoid questions) and show that current LLM-based QA systems often choose incorrectly or average contradictions \cite{liuOpenDomainQuestion2025}. Complementary retrieval-aware stress tests such as RARE introduce controlled perturbations at both the query and document levels and reveal brittleness under document changes and multi-hop settings \cite{zengRARERetrievalAwareRobustness2025}.

Temporal robustness is equally critical: world knowledge changes faster than model weights. Time-sensitive and streaming benchmarks stress whether systems can keep up with new information, including WikiData/Wikipedia-derived temporal QA \cite{chenDatasetAnsweringTimeSensitive2021}, news-stream evaluation in StreamingQA \cite{liskaStreamingQABenchmarkAdaptation2022}, the continually refreshed RealTime QA \cite{kasaiRealTimeQAWhats2024}, and FreshLLMs/FreshQA \cite{vuFreshLLMsRefreshingLarge2023}. Across these settings, retrieval augmentation substantially helps but also introduces new failure modes (e.g., latency, stale indices, contradictory sources).

\paragraph{\textbf{Data Contamination}}
Data contamination occurs when evaluation items—or close paraphrases and answer‐bearing spans—appear in the corpora used for pretraining, instruction tuning, or fine-tuning. Under contamination, test behaviour can collapse into lookup of memorized strings rather than the intended inference or problem solving, inflating leaderboard scores and masking brittleness \cite{ravautComprehensiveSurveyContamination2025a,liOpenSourceData2024}. Empirical work connects such leakage to memorization dynamics: larger models memorize more and earlier in training; duplication and longer prompts amplify verbatim regurgitation; and model capacity allows substantial unintended storage of datapoint-specific information \cite{carliniQuantifyingMemorizationNeural2023,tirumalaMemorizationOverfittingAnalyzing2022,morrisHowMuchLanguage2025}. At the same time, verbatim completion is not conclusive evidence of membership—models can sometimes perfectly complete sequences that were not explicitly seen during training—so naïve $n$-gram or completion-based membership tests can be misleading \cite{liuLanguageModelsMay2025}. Moreover, the metric impact of contamination is dataset- and model-dependent \cite{liOpenSourceData2024}.

Ravaut et~al.\ survey detection methods and distinguish settings where candidate corpora are available (\emph{open-data}) versus unavailable (\emph{closed-data}) \cite{ravautComprehensiveSurveyContamination2025a}. Open-data methods include instance- and dataset-level \emph{string matching} (substrings, $n$-grams, edit distance), \emph{embedding similarity}, and \emph{paraphrase detection}. Closed-data methods include dataset-level \emph{performance analysis} (train–test gaps, controlled retraining, temporal comparisons), \emph{membership-inference attacks} (reference-based and reference-free), and instance-level probes of \emph{model memorization} (e.g., prompt engineering, cloze tasks) and \emph{model confidence} (likelihood or hidden-layer probing).

Despite limitations, string/span matching remains the workhorse in practice. It is simple, auditable, and the only approach that routinely scales to multi-billion-document crawls; when training data are inaccessible for closed models, behavioural signals alone cannot certify contamination \cite{liOpenSourceData2024,ravautComprehensiveSurveyContamination2025a}. Li et~al.\ further note that most prior analyses rely on substring matching and that, without training-set access, uncovering contamination from model behaviour is extremely difficult \cite{liOpenSourceData2024}.

For retrieval-augmented and agentic QA, pretraining-only audits are insufficient. At evaluation time, systems may retrieve and quote target items from web or enterprise indices, producing \emph{search-time data contamination} that boosts scores without demonstrating reasoning \cite{hanSearchTimeDataContamination2025}. Even low apparent rates can reorder competitive leaderboards and erode trust when online access is allowed \cite{hanSearchTimeDataContamination2025}. These observations motivate evaluation protocols that pair scalable contamination checks with designs that limit both pretraining exposure and evaluation-time leakage \cite{mialonAugmentedLanguageModels2023,gaoRetrievalAugmentedGenerationLarge2024,ravautComprehensiveSurveyContamination2025a}.


\paragraph{\textbf{Dynamic Benchmarking}}

Most benchmarks for agentic or retrieval-augmented QA remain static and publicly accessible (e.g., HotpotQA), which makes them susceptible to contamination and memorization \cite{yangHotpotQADatasetDiverse2018a,liOpenSourceData2024,ravautComprehensiveSurveyContamination2025a,carliniQuantifyingMemorizationNeural2023,morrisHowMuchLanguage2025}. As models and training corpora grow, leakage can inflate reported capability and mask brittleness. This has motivated \emph{dynamic} evaluation paradigms that resist memorization and better reflect real-world, evolving information needs. Early platforms like Dynabench and Dynaboard supported human-and-model-in-the-loop data collection and hosted live evaluations \cite{kielaDynabenchRethinkingBenchmarking2021a,maDynaboardEvaluationAsAServicePlatform2021,thrushDynataskFrameworkCreating2022}. Other works instantiate dynamic settings in specific environments (e.g., AndroidWorld for interactive agents) \cite{rawlesAndroidWorldDynamicBenchmarking2025} and in time-sensitive QA streams.

Graph-based and procedural generation offer additional control over complexity and evidence diversity. For structured knowledge, Dynamic-KGQA generates fresh yet distributionally consistent QA sets from knowledge graphs, enabling reproducible but non-static test variants \cite{dammuDynamicKGQAScalableFramework2025a}. However, KG updates are periodic, and web-grounded dynamic QA remains under-explored. Our work targets this gap by constructing evaluation instances at test time from current web sources while recording evidence chains and enforcing multi-document grounding.
